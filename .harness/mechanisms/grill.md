# Grill

Pressure-tests one candidate without taking ownership of the answer.

Use it for a goal, decision, assumption, specification candidate, plan, or Dream proposal when deliberate challenge would reduce the chance of committing a weak premise to authority.

## Method

Work one challenge at a time.

Prefer the question with the highest information value:

1. **Outcome** — what materially changes if this is accepted?
2. **Assumption** — what must be true for it to work?
3. **Counterexample** — what plausible case breaks it?
4. **Boundary** — where does it stop applying?
5. **Evidence** — what observation would support or falsify it?
6. **Cost** — what complexity or future obligation does it create?
7. **Reversibility** — what becomes expensive to change later?

After each answer, update the candidate before choosing the next challenge.

Stop when another challenge is unlikely to change the decision, scope, or owning artifact.

## Rules

- Grill scrutinizes; it never decides on the owner's behalf.
- Do not turn it into a questionnaire.
- Do not ask questions already answered by authoritative context.
- Do not manufacture objections merely to prolong scrutiny.
- Route any newly exposed decision to its existing owner.
- Grill never changes a Dream proposal's status; Dream owns its acceptance/promotion boundary.
